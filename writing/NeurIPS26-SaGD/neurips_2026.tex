\begin{abstract}
Knowledge distillation (KD) for large language models aligns teacher and student output distributions but places no constraint on \emph{which input tokens} each model relies upon---its input--output Jacobian.
We show this gap is structural, not incidental: a first-order Taylor expansion of the KL divergence at a perturbed input decomposes into the standard (zero-order) distillation loss plus a term proportional to the Frobenius norm of the Jacobian difference.
Standard KD minimizes only the former, permitting students that match outputs yet exhibit arbitrarily different input sensitivities---an $L^2$ approximation where a Sobolev $W^{1,2}$ guarantee is needed.
Computing the full Jacobian ($V \times L \times d$ elements) is intractable for billion-parameter models.
We observe, extending a result of Srinivas and Fleuret, that evaluating KL on Gaussian-perturbed embeddings \emph{implicitly} recovers the Jacobian Frobenius norm, without explicit computation or second-order gradients.
Building on this, we propose \textbf{Saliency-Guided Knowledge Distillation (\sagd{})}, which combines two complementary mechanisms: (1)~\emph{noise-injected KL}, where position-adaptive Gaussian perturbations to embeddings provably match the full Jacobian, and (2)~\emph{saliency-guided sample reweighting}, which allocates more training budget to examples where teacher and student attend to different input tokens---a form of distributionally robust optimization.
On SQuAD~2.0 extractive QA and Dolly-15K instruction following (Qwen3-8B $\to$ 0.6B/1.7B), \sagd{} improves over standard KD and six recent baselines in both task metrics (Exact Match, Token F1, ROUGE-L) and a new diagnostic: \emph{evidence concentration}---the fraction of input saliency on answer-span tokens---which \sagd{} brings significantly closer to the teacher's, confirming that the student preserves the teacher's reasoning pattern, not merely its outputs.
\end{abstract}
